# Please, sir, may I have some HTML?
#tech #rant

I'm so tired of the walls between me and the web. I'm increasingly unable to use it due to protective measures.

Today Gitlab rate-limited (banned) me after one (1) pageview. I can't know for sure why any of these sites ever think I'm suspicious, but in this case my guess is it was because the browser is a little bit old, and so it looked a little bit unusual — and we can't trust anything unusual.

Github cuts me off randomly, and will reliably do so if I browse the site at my normal rate — like if I have the temerity to look at the second page of search results. A ton of sites treat me like a criminal for using the search facility. I guess search is expensive, and almost everyone only glances at the top few, so I must be a robot. Google CAPTCHAs me constantly — especially after I turn on verbatim search (which is the only way to get any useful results).

In some cases I'm not being singled out — they treat *all* visitors like this. Some sites won't load at all due to Cloudflare protection or the cryptominer of the week.

Cloudflare CAPTCHA works like this: they provide a button that rolls a die to decide whether to a) let you in b) throw an error, refresh the page, and then let you in c) endlessly refresh the page, pretending it will one day let you in, but it's a lie and you should give up after the second refresh. It is a cruel and capricious digital god.

Anubis and co. sometimes take several minutes to complete. When this happens on my phone, I close the page instead of wasting the battery. (There's a good chance Android will decide to kill the process anyway if I leave it running.) On slow devices, like the old tablet I use for reading comics, these challenges can be an infinite sentence.

I get *why* it's like this. There's a lot of abusive traffic that's hard to deal with. A lot of it looks like legitimate traffic, so you have to ration your pageviews and seize on anything that stands out. But it sucks. It feels like being punished for something you didn't do.

(Then, of course, there are the login walls. These are as often cashgrabs to exploit the data sites are hoarding as they are a protective measure. Reddit put one in place recently, so I just don't go on Reddit. I never liked it much anyway.)
