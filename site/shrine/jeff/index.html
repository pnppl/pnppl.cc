ABOUT

I've been singing these songs for most of my life. They are familiar and close to my heart, like your favorite threadworn hoodie with thumbholes that fit just right. With "Bomb the Music Industry!" written on the back.

I've selected one song I love from each album and written a little about why I like it, what it means to me, and whatever else crossed my mind. I can't say they're definitely my favorites — ranking music is not the goal — but they're songs that I love.

If you've never listened to Jeff Rosenstock's music before, you might hear a song you fall in love with.

If you've heard some of his music and like it, but are feeling overwhelmed by how many albums he has to explore, this might help you figure out where to start. Songs are arranged, roughly, from newest to oldest.

If you've already heard it all a million times, my reflections might still interest you. If that's you, definitely send me your thoughts!

JEFF ROSENSTOCK

Jeff Rosenstock is my favorite musician. Here are some reasons.

He takes the punk ethic seriously

Jeff embraced free culture back in the dark ages and never stopped. He founded his pay-what-you-want, Creative Commons digital record label Quote Unquote Records in 2005. In 2005, Oink's Pink Palace was in full swing, Metallica's destruction of Napster was still relevant, and Radiohead wouldn't release In Rainbows for another two years. To this day, he still shares his music freely via his label's website.

His approach to music is thoroughly anti-capitalistic. He is deeply concerned with how art is compromised by our dystopian economic system and with how he can fight the entropy. Instead of just selling t-shirts, his breakout band Bomb the Music Industry! invited fans to bring their own shirts to be stenciled. The entire edifice of Quote Unquote Records is a monument to the value of Doing It Yourself: now his art has spread so far that there's some weird stranger writing about him on the internet!

He cares about listeners — and the social production of art, and equality in general — far more than most musicians. He has two uncompromising requirements when he plays a show: it must be accessible to all ages, and he starts playing on time. Starting on time might not sound like a big deal if you haven't been to a concert, but it's so unusual it's almost bizarre. It's de rigueur to start hours after the scheduled time. (Yours truly missed an entire set. Her friends didn't believe her.)

His lyrics are personal, sincere, beautiful, raw, and extremely catchy

You may be surprised after the above to learn that his lyrics are not often overtly political. He doesn't shy away from it by any means, but that has never felt to me like the beating heart of his music, which is just how real he is. He is entirely unpretentious. He does politics in the way he approaches music, but he only sings about it if he has something to say.

Of course, this only holds for a limited definition of "politics". You can't actually separate politics from daily life, a fact illustrated starkly over the last decade as fascists have massively fucked up our lives. Jeff sings about that — the horror, fear, anger, despair, dissociation, and guilty pleasures that accompany existence in $YEAR — and this existential edge is what really makes the songs connect on a more universal level. He is speaking from his perspective through his own reality window, but we are all looking out on the same world.

He does a variety of styles

Some musicians just do the same thing year after year. Others have the opposite problem, always reinventing their style but never developing the core sensibilities that listeners can appreciate across genre gaps. Jeff is one of the blessed artists who land in the sweet spot. He is always experimenting, but he's also pretty much always nailing it.

While his ska days are mostly in the past, the dedicated listener will sometimes be rewarded by surprise bits of it. In 2021 we even got an entire ska album! But if you are one of those benighted souls who doesn't love to yell PICKITUPPICKITUPPICKITUP, his post-BTMI ouvre has minimal skanking. It had already mostly receded during BTMI: if you are open to a little pinch of ska, give it a try; it's much more electronic than brass.

His voice is wonderful

He doesn't have a conventional "good singer" voice, which turns some listeners off. Personally, I love it. I like singers who let loose and sing hard. I'm here for emotion and texture, not sterile pitch-perfect melody. I like yelling, screaming, and voice cracking. But he can sing gently, too, to my ears.

MAKING THIS PAGE

This page is a tribute to the Quote Unquote Records website. Its design is very '00s and uses tables and a bunch of images (JPEGs!) to pull off its tricks. It's somewhat broken and neglected, but it still looks both good and interesting and it works flawlessly.

I thought it would fun to recreate it with just CSS. Because I'm relying on modern features, QUR's original tried-and-true site wins out for compatibility. My special effects work with JavaScript disabled, at least! The only JS used is a tiny bit to make the songs work like a playlist, and it's optional.

I've always appreciated QUR's design for its bulletproof simplicity: a list of albums, each of which links to lyrics and liner/release notes which contain links to the mp3 for each song and a full album zip. It's about perfect. I must have downloaded some of these dozens of times over the years when I didn't have my music library handy. My take on it also has everything on one page with notes and download links, but also a few modern extravagances.

TO LEAVE OR DIE IN LONG ISLAND (2005): Syke! Life is Awesome!

						

							

							Download mp3

							
THOUGHTS?
								
I wrote this after listening through the album in the shower --- while drinking a showerbeer, of course. Thank you, Jeff, for sharing that nugget of problem drinking genius! Right after Showerbeers, you're hit with another song about drinking: the downbeat Stand There 'Til You're Sober. Now that you're sloshed, you're in perfect form to belt out this opus. I almost picked it! But once you listen all the way to the end, there's really no other choice. It has to be Syke! Life is Awesome!.

This song epitomizes Bomb the Music Industry!. It's extremely fun and ferociously optimistic while still couching all its enthusiasm in negative terms. This is the rare song that can pierce the shell of your depression. It celebrates life without sugarcoating it. It is determinedly optimistic.

This song is so sweet. I always thought he was talking about a close friend in the closing line, but reading his notes, it was actually about meeting a musical hero.
